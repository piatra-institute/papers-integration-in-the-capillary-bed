# Research

This record audits external sources, not the archived ideation. No other PIATRA paper was used. Registry records were retrieved with curl before bibliography insertion on 14 November 2026. The parameter inventory distinguishes observations, published model choices, and new design assumptions; a literature range is not an empirical confidence interval.

## Primary physiological constraints

- **T1, Longden et al. (2017), DOI 10.1038/nn.4533.** Full text inspected at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5404963/. Results, “Elevation of K+ around capillaries evokes retrograde electrical signaling to the SM of upstream arterioles,” and Fig. 2: capillary application of 10 mM extracellular potassium produced an arteriolar hyperpolarization of 23.2 ± 1.8 mV, with 0.24 ± 0.03 s onset latency, at a recording site approximately 500 μm distant. These are preparation-level summaries, not a digitized single-cell response curve. The stimulus covered an incompletely specified collection of capillary cells. Therefore a model using a smaller stimulated population cannot legitimately fit these means as if geometry were known. Fig. 2 and the online methods distinguish ex vivo Ba2+ application from in vivo surface treatment. The paper supports the existence of the pathway and a voltage/distance reality check, not a unique junctional resistance.
- **T1, Moshkforoush et al. (2020), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2000151117.** Full text inspected at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7368319/. Fig. 2F reports capillary Kir coefficient 0.18 ± 0.10 nS/mM^0.5, background conductance 0.06 ± 0.04 nS, and mean capacitance 8 pF; Methods describes a resting potential near -30 mV. The conductance coefficient differs from conductance at resting extracellular potassium by a factor of sqrt(3). Fig. 3 and Methods explore capillary coupling resistance from 10 MΩ to 1 GΩ because it had not been measured. These ranges concern model plausibility, not parameter identification. Model I, Eqs. 1–3, supplies the membrane-current law. The published Fig4C.m source supplies constants and implementation details unavailable from the inaccessible supplement.
- **T1, Isaacs et al. (2024), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2405965121.** Full text inspected at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11648664/. Results, “A Mouse Model to Study Pericyte Electrical Signaling,” reports 7.6 ± 1.3 pA ArchT outward currents. “Thin-Strand Pericyte Hyperpolarization Dilates Upstream Arterioles” and Fig. 2 show local pericyte stimulation and remote diameter responses. Fig. 4 and subsequent genetic/ablation experiments support KATP involvement in neurovascular coupling. Discussion notes that Ba2+ may act on pericyte Kir and other cells, so a global blocker effect does not localize a distributed amplifier. ArchT is a proton pump, not a KATP channel: its current is only a scale check for an effective hyperpolarizing input.
- **T1, Hariharan et al. (2022), DOI 10.1016/j.celrep.2022.111872.** Full text inspected at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10187957/. Results and Fig. 4 describe pericyte hyperpolarization after KATP activation by pinacidil or reduced glucose; Fig. 2 connects focal pericyte activation to the upstream arteriole and examines Kir blockade. The present reduced endothelial model does not estimate pericyte junctional resistance or ATP/glucose kinetics from these results.

## Closest modeling and experimental precedents

- **T1, Moshkforoush et al. (2020), Figs. 3–7 and Discussion.** Prior work already includes nonlinear Kir sensing, regenerative propagation, graded recruitment of a network, and summation of multiple activated regions. Main text explicitly refers to summation of binary regional inputs in SI Fig. S7; the supplement itself was not inspected. The new contribution must concern held-out paired prediction, fair rival fits, residual decomposition, and a quantified discriminatory measurement, not discovery of capillary electrical integration.
- **T2, Longden (2026), DOI 10.1152/ajpcell.00065.2026.** Full text inspected at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13186272/. “Arithmetic operations in CCUs” and Fig. 4 propose coincident input summation across branches, gain changes, shunting, and vetoes. “Future Directions” proposes multiple electrodes in isolated bifurcating capillaries. Those conceptual and experimental ideas precede this manuscript. The model here gives conditional effect sizes and model-discrimination criteria for part of that program; it does not establish the review's broader framework.
- **T1, Postnov et al. (2015), DOI 10.1007/s11538-014-0058-0.** Full text inspected at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4303742/. “Regenerative Pulse Transmission and Whole-Cell Electric Properties” and “Bistability Hypothesis” identify negative differential conductance and bistable endothelial dynamics as mechanisms for regenerative conducted responses. This rules out claiming a new mechanism merely because a network exhibits a threshold.
- **T1, Rivers (1997), DOI 10.1152/ajpheart.1997.273.1.H310.** Crossref abstract inspected; full text unavailable in this audit. It reports cumulative and saturating responses to multiple methacholine inputs, including dependence on whether stimulated regions belong to one or adjacent arterioles. This is prior evidence for spatial dependence and saturation in conducted vasodilation, in hamster cheek-pouch arterioles. It does not demonstrate cerebral capillary pair integration.

## Strong simpler mechanisms

- **T1, Kapela et al. (2018), DOI 10.1111/micc.12429.** Full text inspected at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5809195/. Table 1 and Discussion, “Endothelial Rgj,” quantify cable-model estimates in arterial endothelial tubes. Junctional resistance depends on cell geometry and arrangement. Their fitted resistances cannot be presented as measurements in cerebral capillaries.
- **T1, Behringer and Segal (2012), DOI 10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.111.262592.** Full text inspected at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3467972/. Results, “Effects of SKCa/IKCa activation on electrical conduction over distance,” shows membrane shunting reducing conduction despite a linear current-voltage relation, including after matching local responses. A pharmacological reduction in remote voltage is therefore insufficient by itself to demonstrate distributed nonlinear processing. The comparison must include measured local inputs, electrical residuals before diameter conversion, and post-intervention single-input refitting.

## Evidential boundary

No raw experimental traces were acquired or digitized. Single-site calibration in the manuscript is a synthetic identifiability and model-discrimination exercise constrained by published magnitudes. Geometry, sampled pulse schedules, noise assumptions, readout mechanics, and bounds not directly measured are documented as assumptions. No paired physiology is held out from an animal dataset: the held-out cases are simulation protocols. Demonstrating that the proposed mechanism can generate a residual under these assumptions does not identify the mechanism used by an intact brain.


## Paired-input and cable precedents added after the referee reports

Wessel, Kristan and Kleinfeld (1999), DOI 10.1523/jneurosci.19-14-05875.1999, demonstrated Kir-dependent supralinear summation of paired excitatory inputs in the leech anterior pagoda cell. Their full-text Fig. 7 and accompanying modeling section use one- and two-compartment models with Kir as the sole voltage-dependent conductance. The Discussion extends the interpretation to inputs on the same versus separate dendritic branches. The paper therefore anticipates the general Kir-summation mechanism but does not supply the capillary matched-single geometry null developed here. Its input depolarizes and deactivates Kir, whereas the present capillary model recruits Kir during hyperpolarization. Rall (1967), DOI 10.1152/jn.1967.30.5.1138, is the classical input-distribution precedent; only bibliographic identity was recoverable in this audit, so no specific derivation or numerical result is attributed to an inspected passage.

Crane, Neild and Segal (2004), DOI 10.1080/10739680490457836, found enhanced conducted hyperpolarization relative to a passive prediction in hamster cheek-pouch arterioles (registered abstract inspected). Diep et al. (2005), DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.2005.090233, modeled the dependence of vascular electrical spread and temporal filtering on cell orientation and coupling (full-text Results and Discussion inspected). Hald et al. (2012), DOI 10.1016/j.bpj.2012.01.055, showed that an exponential conduction profile can fit nonlinear responses and that length constants depend on stimulus and observation time (full-text Discussion and “Reaching steady state” inspected). Segal (2015), DOI 10.1159/000439112, explains why opening membrane channels can dissipate conducted signals despite intact junctions (endothelial-tube discussion inspected). Together these works motivate the paper’s electrical controls and caution against interpreting dilation or spatial decay as a mechanism by itself.

Harraz et al. (2018), DOI 10.7554/elife.38689, Fig. 6C and its Results paragraph, report PGE2-induced reciprocal TRPV4 activation and Kir suppression at room temperature with exponential time constants of 4.9 and 6.4 minutes. Their protocol uses a high-potassium bath and pharmacological manipulation; it is evidence for slow reciprocal modulation, not a calibrated reset after the potassium stimulus modeled here. Moshkforoush et al. (2020), Methods “Modulation of Kir and TRPV4 Currents by PIP2,” adopts exponential current changes on this minutes scale in Fig. 4E and identifies stochastic TRPV4 or delayed currents as possible resetting mechanisms. Any faster phenomenological adaptation test must identify its rates as model assumptions and retain negative outcomes.
