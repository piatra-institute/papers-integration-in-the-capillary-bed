# Brief

## Question

Can a literature-constrained electrical model of a cerebral capillary network produce paired-input responses that strong simpler models, calibrated on single-site responses alone, cannot predict? Which spatial, temporal and pharmacological measurement would discriminate those mechanisms?

## Claim to test

Distributed voltage-dependent inward-rectifier potassium currents may produce an electrical interaction before the common arteriolar output nonlinearity. A useful contribution must establish when that interaction survives parameter uncertainty and fair single-site refitting, quantify the separation from simpler mechanisms, and report conditions where the mechanisms remain indistinguishable. The result concerns a specified model and a proposed measurement; there are no paired-input physiological observations in this study.

## Kind of contribution

Biophysical modeling, parameter identifiability analysis, held-out synthetic prediction and quantified experiment design. Published observations constrain plausible single-site behavior. Synthetic traces can test structural distinguishability under explicitly stated assumptions, but cannot establish that a mechanism operates in intact brain tissue.

## Strongest serious alternative

Independent nonlinear local sensors followed by passive endothelial conduction and a common saturating vascular output can generate nonadditive flow without distributed electrical interaction. A bounded dynamic filter driven by a calibrated passive cable and refitted channel/coupling ablations test whether simpler models can explain the response class. The filter and driver have separate fitted parameter sets. A clean experiment therefore measures electrical responses as well as diameter, includes time-shifted single-input controls, and localizes channel perturbations away from the stimulus sites when feasible.

## Cornerstone literature

Longden and colleagues' capillary potassium sensing and electrical signaling experiments (Nature Neuroscience, DOI 10.1038/nn.4533); Moshkforoush and colleagues' capillary-to-arteriole electrical model (PNAS, 2020); Isaacs and colleagues' pericyte signaling experiments (2024); Longden's computational framework (American Journal of Physiology-Cell Physiology, DOI 10.1152/ajpcell.00065.2026); and primary conducted-dilation and endothelial-cable studies.
